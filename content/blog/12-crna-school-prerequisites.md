# CRNA School Prerequisites

Prerequisite coursework is the requirement most likely to cost you an application cycle, because it is the one you cannot fix quickly. A missing statistics course is a semester. An expired chemistry course taken twelve years ago may be a semester as well. Neither can be solved in the weeks before a deadline.

There is no national prerequisite list. Each program sets its own, and the lists differ in which courses they name, how recent the coursework must be, what grade they will accept, and whether they will take a course from any institution. This guide is about how to read those lists and audit your own transcript against them. Prerequisites are one component of the broader [CRNA school requirements](/blog/crna-school-requirements), alongside licensure, critical care experience and GPA.

## What are CRNA school prerequisites?

Prerequisites are undergraduate courses a program requires you to have completed — separately from your BSN — before you can matriculate, and in many cases before you can apply. They are distinct from admission criteria like GPA or ICU hours: a prerequisite is a specific course with a specific grade, and either you have it or you don't.

Three attributes matter for each one, and applicants routinely check only the first:

1. **Which course** the program names, and which substitutions it accepts
2. **How recently** it must have been completed
3. **What grade** it must carry

A course you took and passed can still fail to satisfy a requirement on attributes two or three.

## Which prerequisite courses do CRNA programs require?

Do not assume a standard list. The useful approach is to collect the actual requirement from each program on your list and look for the overlap.

For a sense of what appears in practice, the CRNA Prep Hub directory records prerequisite coursework for **81 of its 135 programs**. Among those 81 entries, statistics appears in 48, chemistry of some form in 45 (organic chemistry named in 28, biochemistry in 23), anatomy or physiology in 12, microbiology in 12, and physics in 4.

Read those figures carefully. They describe what is recorded in the directory, not what is required nationwide — 54 programs have no prerequisite text recorded, which is not evidence that they have no prerequisites. The numbers are useful for anticipating what you will probably encounter, and useless as a substitute for checking the programs you are actually applying to.

The practical takeaway: statistics and a chemistry course are the two you are most likely to be asked for, so if you have neither, that is where to start looking.

## Chemistry requirements

Chemistry is the prerequisite that causes the most confusion, because "chemistry" is not one course.

Programs may name general or inorganic chemistry, organic chemistry, biochemistry, or accept a choice between them. Some phrase it as an either/or — organic chemistry *or* biochemistry — and some list one as required with another preferred. Some specify whether a laboratory component is needed; some explicitly say no lab is required.

Check, for each program:

- Which chemistry course is named, and whether alternatives are accepted
- Whether a lab component is required
- Whether a course you have already taken satisfies it under a different title
- Whether the requirement is stated as required or preferred

If a program lists a course as "preferred," treat it as a competitive consideration rather than a filter — it will not exclude your application, but it may affect how it reads next to applicants who have it.

## Statistics and other common prerequisites

Statistics appears frequently enough that you should plan on needing it. Programs vary on whether they accept a general statistics course, require one with a research or biostatistics emphasis, or specify that college algebra cannot substitute.

Beyond chemistry and statistics, prerequisite lists in the directory also name anatomy and physiology, microbiology, pathophysiology, pharmacology, research methods and physics — in far smaller numbers, and not in any consistent combination. Some of these will already be part of your BSN, which is why reading the requirement matters: a program may want a standalone course rather than content embedded in a nursing curriculum.

Do not build a plan around a course no program on your list has asked for.

## How recent do prerequisite courses need to be?

Recency rules are real, and they are the reason experienced nurses sometimes discover they need to retake a course they passed years ago.

Recency windows of three, five and ten years all appear in the directory's prerequisite text, so there is no single answer to carry between programs. Some programs state no window at all. Some apply a window to science coursework but not to statistics, or the reverse.

Check three things per program:

- Whether a recency window exists
- Which courses it applies to
- What date it runs from — application date, or the start of the program term

That last point is easy to miss and can move your deadline by a year. A course "within five years of the start term" is a different requirement from one "within five years of application."

## What grades do you need in prerequisites?

Minimum prerequisite grades are set by the program, and specific grade floors — including B and B+ requirements — appear in the directory's prerequisite text alongside course names. Others state no minimum beyond passing, and rely on GPA thresholds instead.

Two things to establish for each program:

- Whether a minimum grade is specified for prerequisites specifically
- Whether the program recalculates a **science GPA** from these courses

The second matters more than most applicants expect. A C in organic chemistry may satisfy a "passing grade" rule and still weigh heavily in a science GPA calculation. Programs weigh cumulative, science, last-60 and graduate GPAs differently, and [how CRNA schools calculate GPA](/blog/how-crna-schools-calculate-gpa) works through why the same transcript produces different numbers depending on the method used.

## Should you retake an old or weak prerequisite?

Retaking is worth it when it does more than one job. A retake that satisfies a recency window, raises a science GPA, and demonstrates recent academic capability is a strong use of a semester. A retake of a course that already satisfies every requirement, purely to convert a B to an A, usually is not.

Work through it in this order:

1. **Does a program actually reject the existing course?** If it is outside a recency window or below a stated grade floor, you have your answer.
2. **What does it do to your science GPA?** Run the numbers before you register — the [GPA Analyzer](/gpa-calculator) will separate cumulative, science and last-60 GPAs so you can see which figure the retake moves.
3. **How does the program treat retakes?** Some replace the original grade, some average both, some count only the most recent attempt. This changes the value of the retake substantially.
4. **What else could that semester buy you?** A semester spent on a course nobody requires is a semester not spent accruing ICU hours or preparing certification.

If the weakness is broader than one course, retaking a single prerequisite will not fix it. [Getting into CRNA school with a low GPA](/blog/crna-school-with-low-gpa) covers what actually shifts an academic profile and what does not.

## Do online or community-college courses count?

This is program-specific and worth confirming in writing rather than inferring.

What to verify:

- Whether the program accepts coursework from a community college for this requirement
- Whether online coursework is accepted, and whether a lab requirement can be met online
- Whether the institution must be regionally accredited
- Whether there are named providers the program will or will not accept

Do not assume the answer generalises. A program that accepts an online statistics course may still require an in-person chemistry lab. When a program's website is ambiguous, email the admissions office and keep the reply — a written answer is what you want if the question resurfaces during review.

## What if you're missing a prerequisite?

First, establish whether it is genuinely missing or merely unrecognised. Course titles change, and a course listed under a different name on your transcript may satisfy the requirement. If you think it should count, ask the program before enrolling in something new.

If it is genuinely missing:

- **Check whether the program allows it to be in progress at application.** Some accept a prerequisite in progress with proof of enrolment, some require completion before the deadline, and some require it before matriculation. These are three different rules and they change what you can do this cycle.
- **Check the term calendar against the deadline.** A course completed in December does not help a November deadline unless the program accepts in-progress coursework.
- **Decide whether it changes your application year.** This is the single most common reason to defer a cycle, and deferring deliberately is better than applying incomplete.

Auditing for gaps belongs at the very start of your planning. In the [CRNA school application timeline](/blog/crna-application-timeline), verifying prerequisites and their recency limits sits in the first phase, eighteen to fifteen months out, precisely because closing a gap takes a semester you have to plan for.

## How to compare prerequisite requirements between CRNA programs

Build a grid before you build an application. One row per program, one column per prerequisite attribute:

| Program | Courses named | Substitutions accepted | Recency window | Window measured from | Minimum grade | In progress allowed? | Institution restrictions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Fill it from each program's own admissions page, and keep a note of the date you checked. Requirements change between cycles, and a grid built two years ago is a liability.

The [CRNA School Directory](/schools) records prerequisite information across the programs it covers, which is a reasonable way to see the shape of the requirement before you commit time to a program. Treat it as a starting point rather than the final word: the program's own published requirements govern, and they can change without the directory knowing.

Once the grid is filled, the pattern usually resolves quickly. Applicants commonly find that one or two courses would satisfy most of their list, and that a single program with an unusual requirement is driving all the extra work — which is a decision you can then make deliberately.

## CRNA prerequisite audit checklist

Work through this before registering for anything:

- [ ] List every program you intend to apply to
- [ ] Record each program's named prerequisite courses and accepted substitutions
- [ ] Record the recency window, and what date it is measured from
- [ ] Record any minimum grade specified for prerequisites
- [ ] Confirm whether in-progress coursework is accepted at application
- [ ] Confirm whether online and community-college coursework is accepted
- [ ] Map your existing transcript against each row, including course titles that may have changed
- [ ] Flag every course that is missing, expired, or below a stated grade floor
- [ ] Calculate your science GPA as it stands, and as it would stand after any planned retake
- [ ] Decide, on the evidence, whether this is your application year

## Frequently asked questions

**What prerequisites do I need for CRNA school?**
There is no universal list — each program sets its own. Statistics and a chemistry course are the two most likely to appear, but you need the actual requirement from every program on your list.

**Do CRNA prerequisites expire?**
Some programs apply a recency window and some do not. Windows of three, five and ten years all appear among programs in the CRNA Prep Hub directory, so check each program individually rather than assuming a standard.

**Should I retake a prerequisite if I earned a C?**
It depends on whether the program specifies a minimum grade, whether it recalculates a science GPA, and how it treats retakes. A retake that satisfies a recency rule and raises your science GPA is doing two jobs; one that does neither is a semester spent for little return.

**Do online prerequisite courses count?**
That is set by each program, and can differ between a lecture course and one with a lab. Confirm in writing with the admissions office rather than assuming.

**Can I take CRNA prerequisites at a community college?**
Some programs accept community-college coursework and some restrict it or require regional accreditation. Verify per program before enrolling.

**Can I apply while a prerequisite is still in progress?**
Some programs allow it with proof of enrolment, some require completion by the application deadline, and some require it before matriculation. Establish which rule applies before you plan a term around it.

**Does a prerequisite grade affect my GPA calculation?**
It can. Programs that compute a separate science GPA typically draw on exactly these courses, which is why a prerequisite grade can matter more than its credit hours suggest.

**Next step:** Build the comparison grid before you register for any coursework. Most applicants discover their gap is narrower than feared, or that a single outlier program is driving the work — and both are decisions worth making with the requirements in front of you.
