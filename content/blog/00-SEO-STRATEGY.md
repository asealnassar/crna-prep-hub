# CRNAPrepHub SEO Content Strategy

## 1. Recommended publishing order

Publish in this sequence rather than by article number. The logic: establish the pillar first so later articles have something to link up to, then front-load the highest-intent pages, then fill in support.

| # | Article | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **CRNA School Requirements** (pillar) | Everything links up to it. Publish first so internal links point at a live page. |
| 2 | **CRNA School Interview Questions** | Highest search volume in the cluster, highest intent, strongest conversion path to the mock interview tool. |
| 3 | **ICU Experience for CRNA School** | High volume, gates the reader's whole timeline, links naturally to the pillar. |
| 4 | **How CRNA Schools Calculate GPA** | Underserved query with strong featured-snippet potential. Direct path to the GPA Analyzer. |
| 5 | **Getting Into CRNA School With a Low GPA** | High emotional intent, long dwell time, strong return visits. Needs #4 live to link to. |
| 6 | **How to Prepare for a CRNA School Interview** | Publish after #2 so the two interview articles can cross-link on day one and differentiate clearly. |
| 7 | **CRNA Personal Statement** | High intent, seasonal spike before deadlines. |
| 8 | **CRNA School Resume** | Same seasonal window as #7; pairs with it. |
| 9 | **Is CCRN Required for CRNA School?** | Narrower query, quick win, supports the pillar. |
| 10 | **CRNA School Application Timeline** | Publish last — it links to all nine others, so it works best when they exist. |

**Cadence:** one per week for ten weeks, or two per week if you can maintain quality. Do not publish all ten at once — a sudden bulk publish of thin-looking pages on a new blog is a weaker signal than steady cadence.

## 2. Pillar page

**"CRNA School Requirements"** is the pillar.

It's the broadest qualified query, every other article is a subtopic of it, and it's the natural hub for internal linking. Treat it as a living document — update it each cycle as program requirements shift, and expand it over time rather than letting it go stale.

Structurally, the pillar should link *down* to all nine supporting articles, and all nine should link *up* to it. That's the standard hub-and-spoke shape and it's what builds topical authority.

## 3. Internal linking map

**Hub-and-spoke plus lateral links:**

```
        ┌──────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┐
        │              │              │              │
   ICU EXPERIENCE   GPA CALC      INTERVIEW Qs   APPLICATION
        │              │              │           TIMELINE

        │          LOW GPA      INTERVIEW PREP
        │              │              │

```

**Specific lateral pairs that must link both directions:**

| From | To | Anchor context |
|---|---|---|
| How CRNA Schools Calculate GPA | Low GPA | "if your GPA is below a minimum" |
| Low GPA | How CRNA Schools Calculate GPA | "which GPA is actually weak" |

**Rule:** every article links up to the pillar, down or laterally to 3–4 siblings, and out to 2–4 CRNAPrepHub tools. No article should be an orphan.

## 4. Keyword cannibalization check

Each article owns exactly one primary keyword with no overlap:

| Article | Primary keyword | Cannibalization risk |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | CRNA school requirements | — |
| 2 | ICU experience for CRNA school | — |
| 3 | how CRNA schools calculate GPA | — |
| 4 | CRNA school with a low GPA | Low — #3 is mechanical, #4 is strategic |
| 5 | is CCRN required for CRNA school | — |
| 6 | CRNA school interview questions | **Watch this pair** |
| 7 | how to prepare for a CRNA school interview | **Watch this pair** |
| 8 | CRNA personal statement | — |
| 9 | CRNA school resume | — |
| 10 | CRNA school application timeline | — |

**The one real risk is #6 vs #7.** They're deliberately separated:

- **#6 = the questions.** Categories, answer frameworks, worked weak/good/elite examples. Someone searching "what will they ask me."
- **#7 = the process.** Six-week plan, program research, logistics, day-of. Someone searching "how do I get ready."

**Enforcement rules:**
- #6 must not contain a preparation timeline
- #7 must not contain a question bank — it links to #6 instead
- Each must link to the other in its opening section, making the distinction explicit to both readers and crawlers

**Similar watch on #3 vs #4.** #3 is "how is it calculated" (mechanical, snippet-oriented). #4 is "what do I do about it" (strategic, decision framework). Keep #3 free of repair strategy and #4 free of calculation mechanics beyond a link.

**Monitor in Search Console:** if two articles start ranking for the same query, consolidate or re-differentiate rather than letting them split authority.

## 6. High-intent opportunities

Ranked by likelihood the reader becomes an actual CRNAPrepHub user rather than a passive researcher:

**Tier 1 — reader is actively applying or interviewing**
- CRNA School Interview Questions → Mock Interview
- How to Prepare for a CRNA Interview → Mock Interview + School Interview Insights
- CRNA Personal Statement → Personal Statement Analyzer
- CRNA School Resume → Resume Builder

These readers have a deadline. Conversion intent is highest and the tool maps directly to the task they're mid-way through.

**Tier 2 — reader is preparing to apply**
- How CRNA Schools Calculate GPA → GPA Analyzer
- Getting Into CRNA School With a Low GPA → GPA Analyzer + Directory
- CRNA School Application Timeline → multiple tools

Slightly earlier in the funnel but highly motivated, and these readers return repeatedly.

**Tier 3 — reader is orienting**
- CRNA School Requirements → Directory
- ICU Experience → Directory
- CCRN → Directory

Lower immediate conversion, high topical-authority value, and they capture people 12–18 months before they apply — which is exactly when the Application Tracker would matter if it existed.

**Strategic note:** Tier 1 articles should carry the strongest CTAs and the most prominent tool placement. Tier 3 should prioritize breadth and internal linking over conversion pressure.

## Implementation notes

**Blocker: there is no `/blog` route.** These articles have nowhere to publish. Building it needs to precede or accompany article one.

**Two tools referenced in the brief don't exist:** Application Tracker and Document Vault. No articles link to them, and none should until they're built — internal links to 404s actively harm crawl efficiency and user trust.

**Verify before publishing.** Every article deliberately avoids specific statistics, acceptance rates, and program-specific requirements presented as universal. Before publishing, confirm that nothing has drifted toward false specificity during editing — that's the most likely place accuracy problems get introduced.

**Schema markup worth adding:**
- `Article` schema on all ten
- `FAQPage` schema on the FAQ sections — strong AI Overview and rich-result potential
- `BreadcrumbList` for the cluster hierarchy
- `HowTo` schema on the Timeline and Interview Prep articles
